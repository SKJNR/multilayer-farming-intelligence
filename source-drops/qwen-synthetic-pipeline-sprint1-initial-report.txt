### PHASE 1: TREE OF THOUGHTS — Designing Metamorphic Relations (MRs)
*Goal: How do we validate the synthetic scenarios and the simulator without real-world field data?*

*   **Branch 1 (Statistical Distribution Matching):** Compare generated weather/soil distributions to historical Anantapur data. 
    *   *Evaluation:* Good for realism, but does not test the *physics engine*. (Rejected as primary validation).
*   **Branch 2 (Cross-Model Verification):** Compare our 2-Layer model outputs against a full 3D Richards Equation solver (e.g., HYDRUS-1D) for every scenario.
    *   *Evaluation:* Computationally intractable for a 5,000-scenario pipeline. (Rejected for Sprint 1).
*   **Branch 3 (Metamorphic Testing - Consensus):** Define invariant physical properties that *must* hold true regardless of the specific parameter draw. If an MR fails, the scenario or the engine is rejected. **(Accepted).**

**The Three Metamorphic Relations (MRs):**
1.  **MR_MassBalance (Turing):** $\epsilon = \Delta S - (\sum In - \sum Out)$ must be $< 10^{-10}$ for every time step.
2.  **MR_DroughtMonotonicity (Aris):** If we reduce rainfall in a scenario, Groundnut uptake ($U_{G1}$) must monotonically decrease or stay the same. It cannot increase during a drought.
3.  **MR_HardpanOverflow (Murphy):** If we set the Hardpan restriction factor to $0.0$ (impenetrable), internal drainage ($D_{12}$) must be exactly $0.0$. Layer 1 storage must eventually hit $\theta_{sat}$ and generate Runoff ($RO > 0$) if rain continues.

def custom_lhs(n_vars, n_samples, seed=42):
    np.random.seed(seed)
